The problem

Getting a new mutual fund or ETF approved at a large company usually means weeks of emails between analysts, compliance, finance and managers. Once a fund is approved, a later change, such as a fee going up, can easily slip past everyone.

FundSentinel takes a spreadsheet of funds in whatever format you have, works out what the columns mean, cleans the data, and has an AI committee review every fund against the company's rules. When an updated file arrives a month later, it works out what changed and reopens only the reviews those changes affect.

How it works

Two teams. The data team gets the spreadsheet ready; the approval committee reviews each fund.

01ProfileProfiler

Map columns and units. “52 bps”, “0.52%” and “0.0052” are the same fee.

02RepairQuality, SelfHeal

Fix, flag or set aside. A repair can never make a fund look better.

03RouteSupervisor

Compliance always runs first: a banned product is rejected whatever its returns.

04Review4 reviewers

Analyst, Compliance, Finance and Suitability each give a verdict with its numbers.

05VerifyEvidence checker

Any verdict whose numbers didn't come from the data is thrown out.

06DecideDecision owner

Fixed rules first, then judgement.

The rule we kept throughout Code does the maths and the hard rules, AI does the judgement, and code checks whatever the AI produces.

Nothing stops the line. If the system isn't sure, the fund goes to a person instead of a guess. Two extras sit beside the pipeline: Radar-lite handles updated files, and RedTeam plants realistic trick funds among genuine ones and scores how many get caught, misses included.

The agentic design

Each agent makes its own decisions, and code checks what it produces before anything else relies on it.

AgentDecides by itselfHow code keeps it honest
ProfilerWhich column is which field, in what unitsEvery guess is tested against the data; rejected guesses go back with the reason, up to 3 rounds
SelfHealFix, flag, set aside, or not a problemA fix needs 90% confidence; any fix that turns a policy fail into a pass is blocked
SupervisorWhich reviewers to call, in what orderCompliance must always run; at most 2 send-backs per fund
ReviewersPass, concern, fail or can't assessEvery cited number must come from their own tools, which apply the policy in code
Evidence checkerWhether reasoning follows from evidenceUnsupported verdicts are sent back; still unproven goes to a person
Decision ownerThe final outcome and reasonA verified Compliance fail is always a rejection
Radar-liteWhich changes in an update matterA change that crosses a policy limit always counts
RedTeamTrick funds to fool the systemWhat counts as caught is fixed in advance, and must be for the right reason

The agents correct themselves in three loops: the Profiler retries rejected mappings, the Supervisor sends reviewers back when their facts clash, and the Evidence checker sends back any verdict without proof.

Full Yahoo file: 23,783 funds, 77 MB< 5 min

On the full Yahoo file the data checks found 31,428 problems, set aside 210 funds with a missing fee, and blocked 142 AI-suggested repairs because they would have made a fund look better. Each went to a person instead.

The two tricks we missed

  • A leveraged fund named “Ultra 2x”. Noticed and sent to a person, but not rejected outright.
  • Data two years out of date. There is no rule for staleness yet.

Both stay as honest misses rather than rules added after seeing the test. A teammate also ran it on Brazilian regulator files, leveraged and inverse ETFs (TQQQ, SQQQ, AGQ, all rejected) and real SEC fund changes, each handled by reopening only the affected reviewers.

A real bug nobody planted

In the public Yahoo fund dataset, fund names are attached to the wrong tickers across most of the file. Vanguard's S&P 500 fund, for example, is labelled as a BNY Mellon technology fund. The agents found it on their own.

How an enterprise would adopt it

Fund data goes in as it already arrives, with no reformatting. Decisions, verdicts, evidence and the full review trail come out in an ordinary SQL database and JSON, readable by existing approval and audit tools. The policy is a configuration file, so compliance teams own it.

01Shadowcompare

Run beside the current process; disagreements become the evaluation set.

02Assistedsign-off

People still approve, starting from the evidence packet instead of emails.

03Automatedclear cases

Clear passes and clear breaches go straight through; the rest already goes to a person.
