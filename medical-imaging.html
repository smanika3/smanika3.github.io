Work / Medical Imaging AI

Swin-CXR-Suite

Swin Transformers, three ways, on chest X-rays: naming thoracic diseases, boxing tuberculosis lesions, and outlining the lungs and heart.

Each task is a separately trained model with its own data, head and evaluation. Classification beats the CheXpert paper's baseline on mean AUC, localization finds active TB well and latent TB poorly (the open problem), and segmentation reaches near-perfect organ masks. All three were trained on Arizona State University's Sol supercomputer.

The task

CheXpert is Stanford's chest X-ray benchmark. Each image carries 14 observations, and the model predicts all 14 at once. Headline scores use the five pathologies on the CheXpert leaderboard: Atelectasis, Cardiomegaly, Consolidation, Edema and Pleural Effusion.

The hard part is the labels. They were mined from radiology reports, so many are marked uncertain (-1) rather than present or absent: about 139k in the training set. Treat every -1 as a target and the loss learns noise; throw them all away and some classes lose most of their positives.

223,414training images, frontal and lateral views
234validation images
668test images used for every reported AUC

Approach

The backbone is Swin Transformer Base (swin_base_patch4_window7_224) with ImageNet-pretrained weights from timm, a 224×224 input and 14 output logits, followed by Dropout(0.5).

A policy per class for uncertain labels

Instead of one rule for every class, each class gets the policy that suits it:

  • Cardiomegaly: U-Ones. An uncertain label is treated as positive (-1 → 1).
  • Pleural Effusion: U-Zeros. An uncertain label is treated as negative (-1 → 0).
  • The other 12 classes: U-Ignore. Uncertain entries are masked out of the loss, so they contribute no gradient.

The masked binary cross-entropy averages only over labels that are 0 or 1. Training uses AdamW (learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 0.1), batch size 96, mixed precision, and flip, rotation, affine and color-jitter augmentation.

Compute

Training ran as SLURM jobs on ASU's Sol supercomputer, one GPU per job, with each job resuming from the latest checkpoint. The script switches to PyTorch nn.DataParallel when more than one GPU is visible. The reported run stopped after 18 epochs. The original training log (Nov 9 to 10, 2025) and test log (Nov 11, 2025) are in the repo, and every number on this page traces back to them.

Peak came early

The mean AUC peaked at epoch 2 (0.9004) and then sat between about 0.873 and 0.892 for the remaining 16 epochs. Extra training gave no net gain. Pleural Effusion is the easiest leaderboard class (0.945 to 0.963); Atelectasis is the hardest and least stable (0.794 to 0.868).

No single epoch is best for every class. Edema, Cardiomegaly and Pleural Effusion each peak at epoch 10, Consolidation at epoch 5 and Atelectasis at epoch 11, so the epoch-2 checkpoint is a compromise across classes.

The reported run masks most uncertain labels out of the loss, so that data goes unused. A supplementary teacher–student experiment, run after the main result, tries to recover it.

Other methods tried

The module keeps every training method from the project as its own script rather than one merged pipeline.

LibAUC AUROC fine-tuning

Fine-tunes a checkpoint by optimizing AUROC directly with PESG and an AUC-margin loss. The script does not run as written: its loss and optimizer calls match no released libauc version.

Baseline and CLI policy mapping

A plain baseline passes -1 straight to the loss as a target. A CLI variant maps uncertain labels per class, but its "U-Ignore" maps -1 → 0 instead of masking. Neither produced the reported result.

A separately trained teacher with its own policies is also in the repo. It was not used for the self-training results above.

Caveats

  • The best epoch was picked on test AUC. Every checkpoint was scored on the test set and the best one chosen from those scores; there was no separate validation-based selection, so model choice and reporting share the same 668 images.
  • The paper comparison is indicative. The paper's numbers are validation AUC; the Swin numbers are test AUC.
  • It is not a pure U-Ignore comparison. The run used U-Ignore for 12 of 14 classes but U-Ones and U-Zeros for Cardiomegaly and Pleural Effusion. The original slides said U-Ignore; the code says otherwise, and the repo corrects it.
  • Self-training is supplementary and also test-picked. The student gains (+0.0014 to +0.0024) are smaller than the teacher's own epoch-to-epoch swing (0.873 to 0.900). They suggest confident pseudo-labels help a little; they do not prove it.
  • The weak classes are weak. Fracture and Enlarged Cardiomediastinum stay below 0.80 AUC at every epoch, so the 14-class model is not uniformly strong.

The task

Classifying a chest X-ray as "TB" is not enough for a radiologist, who needs to know where the lesion is. This module draws bounding boxes around tuberculosis lesions on the TBX11K benchmark and labels each box as one of two subtypes:

  • Active TB: cavitary, infiltrate and consolidation-type lesions.
  • Latent TB: obsolete lesions such as calcified nodules, fibrotic bands and apical scarring.

Most images contain no TB at all, and some patients show both subtypes at once. Both facts shape how the data is split and how the detector is scored.

0.6622best class-agnostic AP50 (epoch 6)
0.5930best FROC score (epoch 11)
0.1905best class-agnostic AP75 (epoch 15)

Approach

The detector is a standard two-stage Faster R-CNN with a hierarchical vision transformer in place of the usual ResNet backbone.

01BackboneSwin-B, ImageNet

512×512 input; features from stages 1 to 3 (256, 512, 1024 channels).

02FPNtorchvision

Lateral 1×1 convs to 256 channels, top-down pathway, four output scales.

03RPNanchors

Anchor sizes 32/64/128/256 px, aspect ratios 0.5/1.0/2.0.

04HeadFaster R-CNN

3 classes (background, Active TB, Latent TB) with a Smooth L1 box regressor.

Training uses AdamW at a learning rate of 1e-4 with no schedule, batch size 8, and the usual multi-task loss (RPN and ROI classification plus regression). The reported run covers 16 epochs, and every checkpoint was scored on the same 1,977 test images. At inference, boxes need a confidence above 0.5 and an IoU of at least 0.5 to count.

A split built for rare classes

A random split would leave the test set with only a handful of Latent or mixed cases. Each image is assigned one of four categories from its boxes (No_TB, Active_TB, Latent_TB, Mixed_TB), and the official training set is split about 70/30 within each category, with at least 20 Latent and 5 Mixed images guaranteed in test. Validation is the official TBX11K validation set (1,800 images).

The open problem: Latent TB

Active TB is detected well. Latent TB is not: its AP50 ranges from 0.0042 to 0.1811 across epochs, and its AP75 never passes 0.0476. The gallery above shows why the per-class number is so low: latent lesions are often located but labeled Active.

Too few examples

The model trains on 73 Latent TB images against 332 Active TB images, with 4,200 healthy images alongside. The test set holds 30 Latent images, so per-class AP is also noisy.

Still climbing

Latent AP50 reaches its best value at epoch 16, the last checkpoint, while Active TB peaked at epoch 6. The best Latent checkpoint is not the best overall checkpoint.

Closing this gap is the obvious next step. The repo does not yet test remedies for the class imbalance, so that work remains open.

The task

Given a chest X-ray, label every pixel as one of four classes: background, right lung, left lung or heart. Clean lung and heart outlines are the starting point for measurements like the cardiothoracic ratio used to flag an enlarged heart.

The labels come from CheXmask, a public set of anatomical masks for CheXpert images, stored as run-length-encoded strings in one CSV. CheXmask ships its own quality estimate per mask (Dice RCA (Mean)), and only masks scoring 0.7 or higher were kept.

130,614training images
55,978test images
0.9619mean test Dice, epoch 11

Approach

The model is Swin-Unet-V2: a hierarchical Swin Transformer encoder feeding a U-Net-style decoder. The decoder upsamples with patch expanding layers (2x per stage) and joins each stage to the matching encoder features through skip connections (concatenate, then a linear projection). A 1x1 convolution produces the four class maps.

01Decodedata/prep_mask.py

Drop masks with RCA below 0.7, then decode the RLE strings to PNG masks at native resolution across a process pool.

02Splitdata/split_train_test.py

Random 70/30 image-level split of the CheXpert train images (random_state=42).

03Traintrain.py

224x224 inputs, CrossEntropy loss, mixed precision, per-class Dice tracked on validation each epoch.

04Evaluateevaluate.py

Per-class Dice on the 55,978-image test split.

05Auditanalysis/min_max_analysis.py

Rank test images by organ-mean Dice and plot the best and worst.

Training used AdamW (learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 0.05), batch size 96, for 11 epochs on ASU's Sol cluster. Encoder depths are [2,2,2,2], decoder depths [1,2,2,2], drop path 0.1. Validation is the 199 images in the CheXpert valid/ set that have a CheXmask mask.

Failure audit

The three lowest-scoring test images are not hard anatomy. They are inputs that are barely chest X-rays at all.

RankInputRight lungLeft lungHeartOrgan mean
#1Noise-filled image0.62190.01270.63360.4227

Two things follow. First, the tail of the error distribution is driven by corrupted or off-target inputs, not by the model missing anatomy on real chest films. Second, the ground-truth column still draws full lungs and a heart on a blank frame, so some CheXmask labels that pass the RCA filter are not real anatomy. A production version would need an input check that rejects non-chest images before segmentation.

Caveats

  • The split is not patient-independent. Train and test are a random 70/30 split of individual images from the CheXpert train set, so images of the same patient can appear in both. Test Dice is likely optimistic for unseen patients.
  • The headline mean includes background. 0.9619 averages four classes; the organ-only mean is 0.9541.
  • Labels are filtered, not verified. Masks are CheXmask's, kept when their RCA quality estimate is 0.7 or higher, with no manual review.
  • Dice is pooled per batch and averaged weighted by batch size, so reproducing the exact numbers needs batch size 96.
  • One run, 11 epochs. Validation loss was still near its low and Dice still creeping up, so a longer schedule might score higher. That was not tested.
